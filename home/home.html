The (new!) Annual Timorous Deep Monkey Run

First things first - the rebuild is going great & PD seems to be adjusting well to his new role as raid leader! We're pleased to welcome many new members and we're still actively recruiting, so please check the forums and toss in an application if you're interested!

Recently, summer doldrums took hold and I found myself in the library. I'm a great fan of nature and a book on monkeys caught my eye. I'm a great fan of monkeys, so I picked it up and started flipping through, looking for something interesting.

Near the end of the book, I came across pictures of a lush tropical paradise, docks sitting above the sea with colorful parrots perched along the huts. It looked relaxing... it sounded like a great place to vacation!

The author wrote that every year, the monkeys migrate from all over the world to this tropical beach paradise...some even making themselves floatation devices out of sticks and leaves, and paddling across great bodies of water.

Why such dedication? Norrathian Primatologists theorized that the migration had nothing to do with mating, but rather, a hunt. They were not quite sure what was being hunted, however.

The writer went on for some time about the mystery of the Timorous Deep migration, but his research was inconclusive. He'd evidently been enjoying the local pirated rum instead of sticking to his research. Indeed, the book ended rather ungracefully after that point, as though he'd given up getting out of the hammock all together.

Disappointed, but my curiosity piqued, I left the library and headed down to Sinking Sands, determined to ask around and get to the bottom of the story. I attempted to interview a monkey, but he declined comment and tried to pull my cloak over my head. I then headed for the source.

The locals in Timorous Deep weren't talking. The best I could glean was that yes, "monkeys come here." Everytime I asked a question, they offered me a drink of their famous Pirate Rum. I managed to finagle free refills at a bar one night until I could barely sit upright, just asking monkey questions.

I really enjoyed my vacation, but I could see why the monkey book crashed and burned.

A month later, I received a note from my friend, Sault, begging for a "care package from civilization." She loved her new tropical home, but they were lacking in a few things she missed. As I was packing up the box, an idea struck - back to Timorous, again, and this time I was loaded down with fresh roasted coffee, a couple cows, some cheese and a large bag of fresh baked buns.

I headed for the pirate caves, the booty in tow, and braced myself for hard bargaining. It turned out to be easier than I expected - they saw the cows and the buns and fell at my feet. If you've been living on raptor and chokidai jerky for months, the idea of a good fresh hamburger is pretty compelling. Out came the rum! This time, I turned it down, and gathered all the information I could. They weren't terribly helpful, but they did tell me when and where I could expect the monkeys. The chap in the back knew a fellow who'd taught his pet monkey to sign - the monkey kept pointing at the bananas and signing "catch banana." Of course, the guy assumed it meant the monkey wanted to play catch with the banana, so he'd just throw a banana at him. Eventually the monkey just disappeared all together.

I gathered my information and went home to plan. It sounded like the hunt was for a banana... perhaps one of unusually large size.

I chatted with a few friends - we decided we would disguise ourselves and run WITH the monkeys and find out first hand. It'd be fun, if maybe exhausting, and who knows what we might see!

The day arrived and we met in the caves off the docks at timorous deep. We encountered no hostile natives, but the word had got out and the press had arrived, along with a couple representatives from the Guides of the Shattered Lands! Curious bystanders stopped to chat, and wish us luck. We went over a few ground rules while I chatted with Leatia the Reporter, from the Norrathian Associated Press.

Some of our adventurers got a little bit excited about their roles the adventure...

Oddly, though we were in the right place at the right time, the number of monkeys was not as high as we expected. We only had one or two real wild monkeys in our midst. Someone pointed out that in the past, people have poached monkeys for sport in Sinking Sands and it was possible their numbers had thinned. This was sad news, as monkeys have always had a special place in the hearts of Holy Mighters. Here we are with the Desert Ambassador to the Primate Races...

His influence in the guild sparked a brief era of monkey-love...

I was then horrified to be informed that even members of our guild had once participated in such atrocious acts - some even playing a game called "Shock the Monkey." I was shown a photo of one guild member who had to be forcibly rehabilitated from her monkey killing habits.

Something about the photo was suspicious, but nevertheless, we had two monkeys in front of us, clear evidence of thinning numbers. It was alarming and we revived our spirits with vows to help the monkeys repopulate. We decided to carry on with our event and run with the monkeys!

With the formalities of the event hammered out and our scout spotters in place throughout the Deep, we headed out!

Taped footage can be found here (right click). We were astounded by these monkey's ability to float through the air, and delighted to find that we could, too!

We followed our two wild monkeys throughout the deep, and eventually we were led into a cave. Up upon a rock, standing in the midst of a waterfall, we caught a flash of yellow. The wild monkeys were off like a shot, and we quickly followed, scrambling on top of the rock to find... a small bright yellow arasai...wait, what?

The wild monkeys stared at her in rapt fascination. We gathered around, confused. We were just chatting with Leatia and filling her in on our discovery, when the fae disappeared - out of thin air. The wild monkeys were off like a shot, and with a few embarrassed words to the reporter, we headed off in pursuit.

As usual, shiny things managed to distract us all, and there was a brief frenzy at the Tunare Shrine.

The frolic at the shrine must have been reported to the authorities...as we ran, a loud bell rang out and we heard shouts...

Then another shout rang out, and we were on the chase again. Doomstrike managed to beat the wild monkeys this time, and we found our little yellow arasai tucked under a bridge near the pirates. A closer look and we realized...she was one of our own!

"What is going on!?" I demanded, and she told us the story.

Years before, she had been in the Deep and had witnessed the running of the monkeys herself. As we'd guessed, there was a great large banana that the entire troupe would hunt down and devour. They would feast and play, and then commence with their courtship and mating rituals before heading back to Sinking Sands. She happened to be in the Deep the next year, and followed the running of the monkeys a second time. This time, however, something went wrong. The banana went bad - or possibly had been poisoned. Hundreds of monkeys died at her feet.

She was horrified! She managed to find a couple survivors and took them back to Maj Dul to be rehabilitated. They were set free again. She returned to the Deep and destroyed the plants, and vowed from then on to return every year and "be" the banana, so the monkeys would continue to migrate and mate.

She carried sacks of bananas with her, dyed her wings bright yellow, then hid and waited. When the monkeys arrived, she'd drop the bananas all over, and escape. Continuing the ritual, but ensuring the bananas were not poisonous.

We were impressed with this story, and her dedication. We applauded her efforts and after delivering the bananas and parting ways with our two wild monkey friends, dragged her back to Qeynos to parade the streets. We met other monkey enthusiasts!

Eventually we returned to Sinking Sands, where we encountered the two wild monkeys, already back and looking smug!


Of course, typical Holy Might style, duels broke out and the evening came to and end with the sounds of swords ringing in the air.

Thanks for joining the fun folks! Thanks to Senior Guide Squeecha, Guide Foinix and the Norrathian Associated Press for the event coverage! Their story should soon be available at the Guide Program website.

Grats guys on Pawbuster!!

Early on, we'd gone in to work on Paw and got ourselves splattered to the 9 kingdoms. A frustrating part about this fight is that it starts out chaos and continues to feels chaotic. With a mix of short raids, new raiders and raiders in need of decent gear/upgrades, we weren't getting too far & "progress" on the mob didn't seem to happen in any logical order. We'd go in, get one good pull, then it would seem like the fight totally changed the next pull. We'd try something, it'd seem to work, then next pull it would fail horribly. We experimented with different call outs & different AA specs, different pull spots, trying to stay out of the AE, trying to stay in for it. Some of these things made a moderate amount of difference, but not enough to make consistent progress.

To the disappointment of some, I shelved him for a while, hoping to get folks better geared/upgraded & in the meantime, mentally "reset" my perspective of the fight. We finished TPR, went in to visit Venril & started working on Kor Sha, which they've now obligingly cleared up to the Overking (GREAT job on the Twins, guys!).

Finally, last week, I decided it was time to go back and try again.

All the practice as a group, gearing up, farming of TPR (otherwise known as "Disneyland") had paid off & raid dps was nearly double what it has been earlier on (42k guys - excellent!). This time things started to click into place & we started making progress. Tuesday, we went back, and (woohoo!) finally hammered him.

It turned into one of those fights where I was focused so hard on doing everything I could think of to keep burning him over the heals as people died ("f...udge...I forgot miracles!"), that near the end, I got a bit glazed around the edges and didn't even see his hp % OR any messages (good thing I wasn't the one on lever duty!). Getting dumped into the pit totally caught me by surprise. One second fighting, the next falling & thinking "Seriously? :D"

In the end it was a fun fight, and one of the most satisfying kills this tier and, how fun, on April Fools day. But, seriously, he's dead! :D

Grats on your updates for your epics and Fate of Norrath quests, guys & GREAT job!!

Grats HM on Mayong!! GREAT job!!

There aren't many raid mobs that get me sniffly at their demise, but Mayong I had to get up and walk away to get a drink and puff through the teary-eye moment that struck when he went down. I didn't realize I was sweating the RoK release so much till that moment.

Back in June, we were psyched to be in HoS and beating mobs. I remember during the spring my goals being along the lines of: beat Vyemm...get past Gnorbl...at least get to Tarinax before raids are over...

To add to this, we're a casual "hardcore" raiding guild, which means we've condensed our raid time down from the 7 days a week, 7pst till "whenever" schedule we had long ago, to 3 days a week and 4 hours, with the rare "stay later" night. This puts us in a time crunch for banging out content - we often run out of time when we're at the mob we want to work on in longer zones. Many of our raiders work early mornings and come with kids, so they're snoring over their keyboards by the end of raids. Raid end is a mass exodus from ventrilo. :D

Getting caught up from a couple hard hits, much less back on track, has been alternately frustrating to the point of banging my head on the desk, and gratifying when we have those "look what wacky stunt we pulled off!" moments.

So this is to say - beating CMF, then FTH - a lot of hard work and time put in. But nothing was as gratifying as getting back into MMIS and starting to work on it. It's not the last zone to beat for the tier, it's not the "hardest" but it's a big milestone (to quote Teddie) and being able to get SoD completed for players was something I wanted before the expansion hit & those new toys started to become less nifty.

So, getting into MMIS on time, and getting up to Mayong with time to spare turned into a headache as we'd get there just on time for raid end. AAAAARGH.

To add to this challenge was the string of bad luck we'd run into in the zone. Twice we had the entire raid, bar 1 or 2 people, crash to desktop in the middle of a fight (this is due to some sort of bug with the mob charm, I hear.), other times we'd get random linkdeaths, screwed up trash encounters with the ever so loveable chaperons (see Soul's video "Remembrance" for a shoutout to the chaperons at the end), or people would have to log... our chanter's pc imploded on her and she disappeared from game, so Chantelle had to step in and play her chanter alt & learn the timing on Viswin & Cheroon....hell of a way to get your feet wet raiding your alt! We also learned in the process that not all chanters lag alike. o0

Finally things started to come together, we started killing the chaperons and their gargoyle pals all together. Courtesans stopped chewing people up so quickly, blood colossus went down a bit faster (notice I'm not saying that the Nameds in there present a big challenge. Thanks for the digital hamster wheel that is trash killing, SOE...and special blessings to the guy who said "let's have them repop and path for miles!!").

We'd get to Mayong and get a pull or two before running out of time. Enough to get some information gathering done. Another night, another couple pulls, a bit more info. Then another string of bad luck. It started getting to the end of the expansion & I wasn't sure we were going to make it, and started gnawing at the straps. Last week, we came to the difficult decision to camp in the zone and come back the next day to work on him, but ironically, it was a patch night. No luck there!

Finally, this week, we got up to him Thursday, but again ran into trouble with numbers and linkdeaths. We took a pull or two and it was, predictably, messy. We decided to call it a night, people were game to camp in the zone, so we came back Friday night fresh from dinner and ready to go, got up to Mayong, and 3 pulls later he died. Go figure!

It was one of those fights where people just suddenly snapped into their groove (LOVE it when that happens). At 10% I was going crosseyed WILLING Rell to stay up, trying to rationalize that if something nutty happened and we DID wipe, then at least we'd got him to 10%... then 6% ...then some *coughcough* Aussie guy ;) yelling "STAKE HIM!!"

So, a bit unorthodox, but done and in under 6 hours between Thursday and Friday. You guys managed to find a way to beat a Boss on limited time, get your updates and not get clobbered with fatigue for morning rush hour. lol /thumbsup! Soon we'll burn through here fast enough to get it down to under the four hour mark.

What the heck is Gnomedarts?

Well, this site may be the quicker, self explanitory version, German though it may be. (language barrier not withstanding, that's a fun game!)
